# dalica-edit

**Layer L1.** The edit recipe: operation syntax parse/print (unknown operations preserved), pipeline order, the CPU renderer for every tool and effect (the oracle), auto-fix analysis.

- **May depend on:** L0 and `image` (`docs/architecture.md` §2; `cargo xtask layers` enforces it). External: `thiserror`. Tests also use `dalica-scenes` (photos and fingerprints) and `proptest`.
- **Public API** (M6 and M7.1; the renderer and the other operations arrive in M7):
  - `syntax`: `Op::parse(text)` never fails and `op.to_string()` prints exactly `text` back, for every string (`data-model.md` §3.1). `Op::Rotate(Turn)` (`rotate(90|180|270)`, clockwise), `Op::FlipH` (`flip_h()`), `Op::FlipV` (`flip_v()`) are typed; the other operations of §3.1 (`LISTED`: crop, straighten, retouch, autofix, autocontrast, autocolor, fill_light, tuning, trim and the effects §3.1 names) are `Op::Listed { name, params }` with the parameter text verbatim until their M7 modules type them; `legacy:…` entries are `Op::Legacy`, and anything else (a newer version's operation, a typed operation with a parameter it does not take such as `rotate(45)` or `rotate(090)`, damaged text) is `Op::Unknown`, both verbatim. A name is `[a-z][a-z0-9_]*`; parameters hold no parenthesis or control character. `op.stage()`: `Geometry` (rotate, flips, crop, straighten), `Adjust` (every other known operation) or `Skipped` (legacy, unknown).
  - `recipe`: `Recipe::parse(strings)` (as stored, no merging; more than `MAX_OPERATIONS` = 10 000 is `Err`, `security.md` §8) and `print()`; `push(op)` appends with the rules of `data-model.md` §3.1: a rotation right after a rotation merges (a net whole turn removes it), a flip right after the same flip cancels, only the last operation ever merges, so an operation between two rotations keeps both; `pipeline()` gives geometry first in the order it stands, then every other known operation in the order applied, legacy and unknown skipped; `orientation()` is what the rotations and flips add up to on the upright picture; `display(exif)` composes it after the EXIF orientation and `display_size(exif, w, h)` gives the displayed size of a stored `w × h` file.
  - `geometry`: `Dihedral`, the eight rotations and mirrorings (a left–right mirror, then clockwise quarter turns): `then`, `inverse`, `from_exif` / `to_exif` (the same eight as EXIF orientations 1–8), `display_size`, `map_pixel`, and `apply(Rgba8)`, which moves every pixel without resampling (the CPU oracle Save uses). A buffer that is not `width × height × 4` bytes is `Err(BadImage)`.
  - `frame` (M7.1, `data-model.md` §3.3): the coordinate frame of stored rectangles and points (the crop, retouch spots, focal-effect centres, face rectangles), which are stored normalised to the photo after its EXIF orientation and before the recipe's rotations and flips. `Point` and `Rect` (0..1, `(0, 0)` top-left, `x` across, `y` down): `transform(d)` maps them by any `Dihedral`, `to_display(orientation)` and `to_stored(orientation)` convert with `Recipe::orientation()`, and a mapped `Rect` always comes out `ordered()`. Non-finite and out-of-range values map without failing; callers clamp or refuse them.
- **Test it alone:** `cargo test -p dalica-edit` (no window, GPU, network or personal photos needed). `tests/recipe.rs`: every operation of §3.1 prints back as parsed, unknown and legacy operations stay verbatim and in place, the merge and cancel rules, the pipeline order, the limit, and proptest on random strings and recipes (never panics, prints back unchanged, merging never changes the net orientation or any other operation). `tests/geometry.rs`: every recipe of up to four rotations and flips (781) on a `scenes` photo stored with each EXIF orientation equals turning the upright photo step by step with an independent implementation, pixel for pixel, with the right displayed size; the eight results match `tests/fingerprints/geometry-<transform>.json` (`architecture.md` §11; PNGs in `target/test-output/edit/`, `DALICA_BLESS=1` rewrites them). `tests/frame.rs`: proptest over every transform (displayed-then-stored and back give the point or rectangle back; two transforms compose), whole turns and double flips bring every point back, and on real pixels a rectangle on a `scenes` photo stored with each EXIF orientation and turned by short recipes covers the same pixels in both frames.
